## A studio for exploring systems through play

I developed these projects through Missouri Video Game Company LLC, working
across Unity, Godot, and the browser. The collection ranges from small reaction
and incremental games to procedural worlds, industrial simulations, and
multiplayer integrations.

Across the projects, the interesting questions are often about interacting
systems. What happens to a factory when moving material competes with operating
machines? How do choices change a shared fictional world? What if training a
neural network were something a player did to improve an item?

Each case study below describes a different experiment: the game concept, how
its systems fit together, the implementation that exists, and what the available
evidence establishes about its progress. The collection includes prototype work;
it is not a catalog of sixteen released commercial games.

## Simulation, industry, and economics

- **[Fabrication Empire](/projects/fabrication-empire/)** — an isometric shop
  simulation where materials, machine footprints, labor, quality, and deliveries
  affect the business. This is the closest connection between my industry work
  and game development.
- **[MEP incremental game](/projects/mep-incremental/)** — electrical, plumbing,
  and mechanical trades become a progression system of manual work, parts,
  workers, tools, research, and contracts.
- **[Privateer Manager](/projects/privateer-manager/)** — a maritime management
  sandbox built around an explicit simulation tick, autonomous actors, trade,
  and conflict.
- **[BurnBucks](/projects/burnbucks/)** — a small, absurdist browser clicker about
  burning money to earn more money, with purchases that turn manual actions into
  automatic income.

## Multiplayer, learning, and narrative

- **[RimFPS](/projects/rimfps/)** — a Unity first-person client, multiplayer
  server, and RimWorld mod exchanging simulation state across different environments.
- **[Neuralnomancer](/projects/neuralnomancer/)** — a dungeon-game experiment
  that puts neural-network training into equipment mechanics, with a Godot
  client and Rust marketplace infrastructure.
- **[Journal RPG and Ironhaven](/projects/journal-rpg/)** — a narrative game about
  ordinary people in a changing world. The engine handles consequences while
  GitHub-backed storage preserves journals and world state. Ironhaven supplies
  a medieval-fantasy world for that engine.

## Procedural worlds and movement

- **[RekklessRunning](/projects/rekkless-running/)** — connected rooms and
  spline-based geometry, supported by custom tooling and runner gameplay.
- **[DungeonCrossing](/projects/dungeon-crossing/)** — an island-and-dungeon
  prototype combining procedural voxel spaces, destruction, and persistent hub systems.
- **[Dungeon Isle](/projects/dungeon-isle/)** — settlement residents and their
  relationships, injuries, and supplies carried into expedition and combat systems.
- **[Forever Stick Fight](/projects/forever-stick-fight/)** — procedural
  stick-figure motion and combat developed into a biome-crawl prototype with
  drafts, weapons, and touch controls.
- **[StickFighterForever](/projects/stick-fighter-forever/)** — a separate,
  smaller survival-platformer implementation with waves, experience, upgrades,
  and automatic weapons.

## Combat, probability, and reaction

- **[RogueCraps](/projects/rogue-craps/)** — a casino-themed dungeon RPG prototype
  connecting ECS combat, physical dice, procedural dungeon structure, and inventory.
- **[DungeonCraps](/projects/dungeon-craps/)** — dice and zone-capture rules built
  around probability, failure prevention, and repeat challenges.
- **[EFV weapon systems](/projects/efv-weapon-systems/)** — modular weapon,
  inventory, player-health, and AI systems for a Unity FPS prototype.
- **[BooBoo](/projects/booboo/)** — a compact reaction game that rewards booing
  non-puppy images and penalizes booing puppies.

## Reading the case studies

The pages distinguish inspected source, saved development artifacts, and checks
performed during the portfolio review. A test file demonstrates an intended
check; an actual test run or playthrough provides different evidence. Where
features remain planned or partially implemented, the case study says so.

Dates describe retained development work, not public release dates. Similar
names identify separate retained implementations unless their relationship is
established. Screenshots are captioned as prototype or development views.

The wider [project collection](/projects/) connects this game work with my
career, automation experiments, and hands-on builds.
