## Games are a good way to explore systems

I made these projects through Missouri Video Game Company LLC, working in Unity, Godot, and the browser. Some started as small experiments in a single mechanic. Others grew into simulations with economies, procedural worlds, multiplayer state, or tools to help me build and inspect the game.

I like game projects because they make systems interact. A factory needs to move material as well as operate machines. A choice in a story should change the world in a way I can still see later. A neural network is more interesting to me when it changes what an item does, rather than living in a demo screen by itself.

The pages below are written from the implementation I have, including its unfinished edges. These are prototypes and experiments, not sixteen released games. I keep the project notes specific so another developer can see what I tried, how I organized it, and where I would take it next.

## Simulation, industry, and economics

- **[Fabrication Empire](/projects/fabrication-empire/)** — I modeled a fabrication shop where materials, machine footprints, labor, quality, and delivery deadlines all affect production.
- **[MEP incremental game](/projects/mep-incremental/)** — I turned electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work into a progression loop of manual jobs, parts, workers, tools, research, and contracts.
- **[Privateer Manager](/projects/privateer-manager/)** — I built a maritime sandbox around an explicit simulation tick, autonomous ships, trade, cities, and conflict.
- **[BurnBucks](/projects/burnbucks/)** — A deliberately silly browser clicker where each purchase automates another way to burn money.

## Multiplayer, learning, and narrative

- **[RimFPS](/projects/rimfps/)** — I connected a Unity first-person client and multiplayer server to a RimWorld mod, exploring how clients can join and stay aligned with an existing simulation.
- **[Neuralnomancer](/projects/neuralnomancer/)** — I made neural-network training part of equipment, backed by a Godot runtime and Rust marketplace code.
- **[Journal RPG and Ironhaven](/projects/journal-rpg/)** — I built a narrative engine where choices change characters and factions, then used Git-backed files to persist the world. Ironhaven is a setting for that engine.

## Procedural worlds and movement

- **[RekklessRunning](/projects/rekkless-running/)** — I generated connected rooms and spline paths, then built Unity editor tools to inspect and shape the course.
- **[DungeonCrossing](/projects/dungeon-crossing/)** — An island and dungeon prototype where seeded voxel spaces can be destroyed and player state follows you between scenes.
- **[Dungeon Isle](/projects/dungeon-isle/)** — I connected a settlement's residents and relationships to party selection, injuries, supplies, and dungeon expeditions.
- **[Forever Stick Fight](/projects/forever-stick-fight/)** — I experimented with procedural stick-figure movement and combat, then expanded it into a biome crawl with weapons and touch controls.
- **[StickFighterForever](/projects/stick-fighter-forever/)** — A separate survival-platformer prototype with generated platforms, enemy waves, upgrades, and automatic attacks.

## Combat, probability, and reaction

- **[RogueCraps](/projects/rogue-craps/)** — I connected ECS combat to physical dice, procedural geometry, and grid-based inventory placement.
- **[DungeonCraps](/projects/dungeon-craps/)** — A dice-and-zone prototype where probability, failure protection, and repeat challenges shape progression.
- **[EFV weapon systems](/projects/efv-weapon-systems/)** — I worked on modular weapons, attachment statistics, inventory, player health, and AI systems for a Unity FPS prototype.
- **[BooBoo](/projects/booboo/)** — A small reaction game: boo the non-puppies and try not to boo the puppies.

The project dates describe retained development work, not release dates. On each page I distinguish code and artifacts I inspected from gameplay or tests I actually ran. If something is still a stub or a design idea, I say so. Similar names refer to separate projects unless I have evidence that they are the same thing.
